# The Odyssey Years - My Destination Is Freedom

*On poverty, security, youth, and the courage to choose my own life.*

I was born into a poor rural family in China. By many conventional measures, I have come a long way. I have received a good education, secured a respectable position in the public sector, and acquired a degree of social standing and financial security that once seemed far beyond the reach of the child I used to be.

Yet there is something deeply paradoxical about the life I have built.

Almost everything that makes my present life secure is tied to my professional position. My income, healthcare, career prospects, and much of my sense of stability depend on it. Should I lose this position, I would find it difficult to replace the security it provides. I have worked hard to arrive where I am, but the very things that make my life comfortable have also made it difficult for me to leave.

I have long wanted to live somewhere else. The climate where I am now is certainly not what I desire. I dream of warmer weather, more sunshine, and a different environment. Yet I cannot say that I dislike China altogether. There are things about life here that I genuinely appreciate: stability, safety, convenience, and the familiarity of a society in which I know how things work. In some respects, these advantages compensate for what I find unsatisfactory.

I worry that my knowledge may not be sufficient, that my English may fail me, and that my professional skills may not be competitive enough in another country. Most of all, I worry about losing the ground beneath my feet. Without my current job, my ability to withstand financial hardship would be painfully limited. I do not have a substantial family fortune to fall back on. If I were to leave without adequate preparation, I might exchange a secure but imperfect life for one in which even ordinary necessities became uncertain.

This is why I have been hesitant. I want to leave, yet I am afraid of what leaving might cost me. I long for another life, but I fear losing the one I have managed to build.

And time, indifferent to all my calculations, keeps moving.

I know that youth does not last forever. I cannot help wondering whether some people are simply destined to spend their best years this way: longing for a different life, yet remaining where they are because they cannot afford to take the risk. I envy those who seem free to explore the world, to pursue their ambitions, and to make their choices without constantly calculating what they might lose. I do not want my youth to slip away in the shadow of things I am afraid to give up.

Yet I am even more afraid of losing what I have.

Poverty has followed me like a shadow since childhood. It has shaped me in ways I am still trying to understand. It has made me both proud and insecure: proud because I have fought my way out of circumstances that once seemed inescapable, and insecure because I have never entirely forgotten how easily everything can be taken away. It is almost as though poverty has become a part of my nature, a snake coiled tightly around me, tightening its grip whenever I contemplate uncertainty, its fangs forever close to my throat.

I find this deeply troubling. The road ahead is there. I can see that other possibilities exist. And yet I cannot simply take them.

The road I long to leave is also the road that gave me my legs. It was along this very road that I learned to stand, to walk, and eventually to reach where I am today. To step away from it without another ground beneath my feet, I fear, would not be to walk into a new life, but to leave my footing behind and begin again on my knees.

Perhaps this is the central contradiction of my life: I have struggled so hard to escape insecurity that I have become afraid of the very uncertainty through which a freer life might be found.

But I do not want to remain here forever.

## Keeping More Than One Road Open

For the time being, I intend to preserve my professional position while exploring opportunities within China.

I would particularly like to move to a place that better suits my preferences. Kunming and the Guangdong-Hong Kong-Macao Greater Bay Area are among the possibilities I find attractive. I would also consider other cities with a better climate, more opportunities, and a lifestyle closer to what I want.

I am willing to consider different professional arrangements, including positions in public institutions. Working at a university especially interests me. Such an environment might offer opportunities for further study, academic exchange, international cooperation, or even visiting scholar programmes. It could allow me to remain within a relatively secure professional environment while gaining greater exposure to the outside world.

Of course, I do not want to romanticise any particular position. What matters is not the title of the institution, but the actual quality of life and the opportunities it provides.

I also intend to keep the possibility of living abroad open. New Zealand and Australia remain among the places I would like to explore. But I do not want to make migration an impulsive escape from my present circumstances. Before taking that step, I need sufficient savings, stronger language skills, and a realistic understanding of the occupations and qualifications available to me.

For now, I would rather prepare than gamble.

This does not mean putting my dreams on hold indefinitely. It means making them more attainable, one step at a time. I can research overseas opportunities, improve my English, acquire useful qualifications, and experience other countries through travel or study whenever possible. I want to know what life abroad is actually like, not merely what it looks like from a distance.

Perhaps I will eventually discover that the life I want is possible in China, just in a different city and a different professional environment. Perhaps I will find that I belong somewhere else. Either outcome is acceptable, provided that it is a choice I have the means to make.

## The Odyssey Years

I have come to think of this period of my life as my *Odyssey years*.

Like Odysseus setting out on a long and uncertain voyage, I do not know exactly where my journey will take me. I have no perfect map, no guaranteed route, and no assurance that the destination I imagine today will remain the destination I desire tomorrow.

There will be setbacks. Some plans may fail. Some opportunities may turn out to be illusions. I may discover that certain things I once longed for are not quite what I expected. I may also find possibilities that I cannot yet imagine.

For now, I have five things I want to keep at the centre of my life: learning English, accumulating wealth, strengthening my body, enjoying films, music and books, and exploring better possibilities for my career.

These are not separate ambitions competing for my attention. Together, they form the foundation of the life I hope to build.

I want to make progress without becoming consumed by the pursuit of progress. I want to prepare for the future without sacrificing the present. I want to be ambitious without becoming a prisoner of my own ambitions.

Over the next few years, my task is relatively clear, even if my final destination is not. I must build a financial safety net, acquire skills that can sustain me beyond my current position, continue learning, and explore different possibilities with patience and determination.

I do not need to know exactly where I will be at thirty. I only need to make sure that the person I become by then has more resources, more knowledge, more confidence, and more freedom than I have today.

Perhaps that is what this stage of life is for. Not necessarily to arrive somewhere, but to prepare for a journey whose destination is not yet fully visible.

I have spent much of my life trying to secure a place for myself in the world. Now I want to make sure that I am not confined to that place forever.

I do not know whether I will eventually stay in China or build a life elsewhere. I do not know what profession I will have, what city I will call home, or what kind of person I will become along the way.

But I know what I am looking for.

My destination is freedom.

And for now, I must build the ship, gather its provisions, strengthen myself for the voyage, and learn to navigate the unfamiliar waters ahead.

**I do not yet know where the journey will take me. I only know that I must keep moving, and that I want to arrive there by my own choice.**
